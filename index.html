History of the font

The first versions of the STIX fonts were a clone of the Times New Roman Typefaces, produced for STI Pub by MicroPress. However, in 2014, Tiro Typeworks commissioned it to revise the font and repackage the typefaces, resulting in the distinct STIX Two types.

Description: The STIX Two types bridge the gap between sophisticated typographic tools for natural language typesetting and the specialized needs of mathematical and scientific publishing. The first versions of the STIX fonts were a clone of the Times New Roman Typefaces, produced for STI Pub by MicroPress. However, in 2014, Tiro Typeworks commissioned it to revise the font and repackage the typefaces, resulting in the distinct STIX Two types.

In redesigning the types, Ross Mills replaced the Times New Roman clone with a new design that references the original 10pt metal type size of Times, resulting in a more rugged form better suited to a range of print and digital media. The STIX redesigns seem to have a softer stroke on the typefaces and more variables and characters.

Supported Languages include:

The core character set of all the fonts covers pan-European Latin, Cyrillic, and Greek language support, IPA phonetic transcription characters, plus common punctuation and symbols.
